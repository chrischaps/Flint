The wooze field's code is in flint-core (`wooze`), flint-scene (`WoozeDef`) and the player; its note is here beside its main consumer. Shallow wooze and shading boil: `crates/flint-render/CLAUDE.md`. ## Living props

- **Living props** (epic #206) -- a `LivingGenerator` splits a prop into `structure(spec, lineage_seed)` (segments with stable `id`/`parent`, leaf cards bound to segments; a tree's is exactly what `flint gen --seed <lineage>` bakes) and `pose(spec, &skeleton, shape_seed)` (per-segment rotation, length and radius scale, relative to the structure). `tree_v1` supports the built-in L-system only; space colonization and text grammars report themselves unsupported. `form_v1` (`generators/form/`, for Embodiment) is the second: one abstract form swept around a spine of bones, with two-sided cards that boil (scattered, or `card_layout = "rows"` as lines of writing; `body_opacity` below 1 draws the body translucent, 0 not at all, #575), and poses drawn from a seeded curve (`bend_deg`, `ripple`, `swell`, `turn_deg`, `stretch`, and `slide` along the section's broad axis, the one term that doesn't pivot at the base: `SegmentPose::slide`, absolute and not carried to children, zero for trees; full size at `drift.amplitude = 1`) rather than regrown. `pose` is `full_pose` held to `[living] variance` (#229): each segment pulled toward the skeleton by `amount × ((1 − steady) + steady × (depth / max_depth)^1.3)`, then with `keep_size` one shared length scale centres the box's sides on the rest pose's; `LivingSkeleton::with_variance` re-holds a `full_pose` without regrowing, and `VarianceConfig::FULL` is bit-identical to `full_pose`. `[living]` in a `*.procgen.toml` is validated at parse. `flint gen` on a supported spec bakes its skinned rest from `lineage_seed` (#209): bone 0 is the trunk, then one bone per segment, and `LivingGenerator::rig` returns the mesh with `LivingRig::palette`, which turns a pose or a blend into bone matrices. Reach it from a `dyn Generator` with `as_living()`. Book: concepts/procgen.md
- **Living props** (epic #206) -- a `living_prop` component on a model whose asset names a `[living]` spec. `flint_procgen::LivingProps` (owned by `ProcGenResolver`) keeps one skeleton and rig per spec and lineage, and per prop a ring of poses (plus one spare) a few steps ahead of its clock, filled by `process_living` after `process_frame` with whatever's left of the frame budget; poses have their own cache, since the resolver's queue dedupes by spec name. The player rewrites `model.asset` to `<spec>@<lineage>` and sets `runtime`, uploads through `load_skinned_model`, and gives each prop its own bone buffer. Shape seeds come from the entity's *name* plus the step index. `--exit-after` prints `[living]` lines on whether rings stayed full and within budget, and what blending cost. Each frame (#212) `LivingProps::blend_prop` reads every part's place on the warped clock, blends its two poses through `LivingPose::blend_from` (which the strip shares) and writes only what moved through `write_entity_bones`; 40 oaks cost about 0.35 ms. A scene's `[living]` table holds `shadows = "blended" | "drift"` and `echoes`. Leaf jitter fits in the palette: the last `BOIL_BONES` bones hold only an offset, and a card's vertices weight them by `n x r` (`CardBoil`, `LivingRig::runtime_joints`), which glTF forbids, so those weights go only to the renderer and never into the baked GLB. Leaf tints are quantized and set per part with `set_entity_part_tint`, so a grove shares a few materials. Echoes (#228): `[living] echoes = { count = 6, spread = 0.9, alpha = 0.2 }` in the spec, or field by field in a scene's `[living]`; `count = 0` is pixel-identical to none and costs nothing. Each echo is `LivingProps::blend_echo` (the prop's own blend at an offset time) into `write_entity_echo_bones`, which is what makes it exist; a spec's `[living] echo_blend = "lighten"` draws them with a max blend of premultiplied colour (`set_entity_echo_lighten`, `fs_skinned_lighten`), so they can only add light, and `[living] echo_slide = <steps>` gives the poses' slide to the echoes alone (the prop never slides; an echo's grows with its `|offset|`, whole at that many steps; `LivingConfig::slide_shown`, and `DrawnBones::slide` so a hold can't hide a change), and `[living] echo_places = { at = [[along, up, steps], ...], from, whole }` gathers the copies onto a few set places once the spread passes `from` (bit for bit the continuous spread at or below it; one copy a place from `whole`, `EchoConfig::copies_placed`; offsets along `LivingSkeleton::place_axes`, `form_v1`'s its lines and its length, a tree's X and Y; a placed copy's slide fades, `EchoCopy::slide_shown`; `DrawnBones::place`; #610); the renderer draws it front faces only with no depth write, after everything opaque, reusing the entity's transform and sharing materials by content. 40 oaks with 6 each add about 3.7 ms a frame, mostly palettes. With echoes on, `flint gen --strip` draws through the same skinned path. Bench: `demo/living_props.scene.toml`. Book: concepts/procgen.md
- **Living Props knobs** (#218) -- the F3 **Living Props** panel (`flint-debug-ui/src/living_props_panel.rs`, in any scene with a `living_prop`) and Rhai's `tune_living(map)`, `freeze_living(bool)`, `step_living()`, `save_living_preset(name)`, `load_living_preset(name)` share one path, `player_app/living_tuning.rs`: a change is a `flint_scene::WoozePreset` whose unset fields leave a knob alone, so a preset file loads exactly what a person turned. A ring keeps each pose twice (`RingPose`: `full` as it grew, `held` at the lineage's variance) and the cache only the first, so `LivingProps::set_variance` re-holds with no regrowing and reaches cached poses: poses in use across the grove first, then the rest taking turns with filling. While a knob moves, filling goes to the pose needed soonest (`pose_needed_soonest`), ahead of re-holds and of back-poses for waiting clocks; at rest rings fill as they always did, which keeps frames byte-identical. `set_echoes` / `set_stagger` reshape the ring to cover old and new clocks, and hold the new ones as `PendingClocks` until `ring.holds` what they read, so `frames_starved` counts only what's in use (`in_use_start`, not `ring.first`). Hidden echoes keep their buffers (`set_entity_echo_count`, opacity 0). A preset file is `wooze/<name>.wooze.toml` beside the scene or one level up; `load_scene` fills it into the scene's tables (`flint_scene::apply_preset_file`), so everything downstream only sees knobs, and the scene's own values win except for `[boil]` and stagger over a component's. Freeze is `living_dt` 0; a step is `LivingProps::advance_beats` before the tick, a whole beat per prop whatever its step. `demo/living_sweep.scene.toml` moves every knob: its `--exit-after` lines should say 0 frames missing a pose and 0 kept their last bones. Book: guides/debug-panels.md, concepts/procgen.md

## Wooze field

- **Wooze field** (#213) -- `flint_core::wooze::WoozeField::sample(position, time)` returns `{ amplitude, step, glide, jitter }`, the one answer to how woozy a place is. A scene's `[wooze]` table (global knobs, `preset`) and `[[wooze.zone]]` entries (`shape = "sphere" | "box"`, `center`, `radius` / `size`, `falloff`, any knob) become it through `flint_scene::WoozeDef::field`; `flint validate` fails on a bad zone, naming it, through `WoozeDef::check`, and the player warns and goes without it. No table, no field, nothing samples. In a scene with a field it decides `step` and `glide` for every prop, whatever the spec says. Rhai: `set_wooze(map[, seconds])`, `set_wooze_zone(name, map[, seconds])`, `remove_wooze_zone(name[, seconds])`; ramps are what make the field a function of time. A consumer must not pop on a change: count the clock in beats advanced by `dt / step`, and read glide and amplitude through a `flint_core::wooze::Cycle`, which holds what a part started its cycle with until the cycle ends. `amplitude` is applied per part with `SegmentPose::pulled`; `jitter` scales card turns, and goes into tints before they're quantized so materials stay few. Presets: `flint_core::wooze::PRESETS`, `linklater` a placeholder until #219. Schema: `schemas/components/wooze.toml`, which isn't a component. Bench: `demo/wooze_field.scene.toml`. Book: concepts/procgen.md

## Woozy Grove

- **Woozy Grove** (#217) -- `demo/woozy_grove.scene.toml`, the walkable place the look gets judged: 38 living trees from `demo/specs/grove_{oak,cypress,birch,thorn}.procgen.toml` (L-system, no `[[lod]]`), a bench and lantern on one `shallow_wooze`, zones `hush` / `drowse` / `shiver` around a clearing that takes `preset = "linklater"` (#219: amplitude 0.5, step 0.5, glide 0.6, jitter 1, with the scene's `boil_on = 1`; a built-in holds only the field's knobs, so a scene with that preset and the default `boil_on = 2` boils at half the rate). `tools/living-strip/living_strip.py` makes the book's before/after strip and slit-scan from the scene: a slit-scan (one row of pixels stacked over time) shows a lurch, hold or glide in a still, which frame diffs and contact sheets don't. Judge a look's cost by the `[living]` lines, not fps: a grove that has run out of poses still reports 60 fps, each starved prop simply keeping its last bones. Check them on a long run and under CPU load too; a 30 s idle run once showed 0 where a loaded one showed 3,016 of 3,560 frames short, because `fill_within` refused every pose while its estimate sat just under the budget. `demo/woozy_grove_walk.txt` is a route through them for `--input-script`. `crates/flint-cli/tests/woozy_grove_cli.rs` keeps it loading. Book: concepts/procgen.md
