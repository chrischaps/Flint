### `[panels]`: several cameras and looks in one frame, laid out as a comic page (#880)
**What changed.** A scene's new `[panels]` table lays the frame out as panels. Each `[panels.panel.<name>]` has a `rect` (shares of the page), a camera (`position`, `target`, `fov`, or `follow` an entity) and its own `look` / `post_process`. Each panel is drawn at its own size with the full post chain, then laid on a gutter inside an inked border, with the HUD still over the top. `flint render` and the player draw panels. Scripts cut between layouts with `set_panels(map)`, `set_panel(name, map)`, `remove_panel(name)` and `clear_panels()`. `Camera` is now `Clone`. A host that draws frames itself calls `SceneRenderer::encode_frame_to` / `render_frame_to` (they take the world), and `set_panels(layout, def)` at each scene load. N panels are N GPU submissions, and a scene without `[panels]` draws exactly as before. `flint_render::panels` is the API. Demo: `examples/comic_panels.scene.toml`.
**What to do.** Nothing, unless your game embeds the renderer and wants panels. Then replace `encode_to` with `encode_frame_to` and call `set_panels` at scene load.
