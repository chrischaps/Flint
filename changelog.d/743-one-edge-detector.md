### Pencil, contour and ASCII read one shared edge pass; a floor seen edge-on no longer inks a band (#743)
**What changed.** The `[post_process] edge_depth` / `edge_normal` pass (#301) is now where pencil's contours, contour's silhouettes and ASCII's line glyphs get their depth steps and creases. It keeps a step only where the depth really bends, so a floor seen edge-on no longer draws a dark band along the horizon in any pencil look (`graphite`, `blueprint`, `charcoal`...): the horizon is one line, and figures standing at it read again. Pencil's `depth` and `crease`, contour's `width` and `threshold`, and ASCII's `edge_depth` / `edge_normal` keep their meaning, measured against the shared edges; ASCII's direction follows the scene's thresholds. Pencil's `grazing` still adds its own stricter test. A user style gets `flint_unproject(inv_view_proj, uv, depth)`, `flint_unproject_h` and `flint_safe_w` in its prelude, the text the built-ins use; its other prelude helpers are unchanged. A Rust style can say `.needs_edges_when(f)` or `StyleNeed::EdgeSteps`, and `StylePass::edges_rect`.
**What to do.** Nothing. A look that leaned on the old horizon band reads lighter near the horizon now; there is no switch that brings the band back.
