### Animation is a standard package: `animation = false` drops clips, sequences, `tween` and the `animation` module (#1620)
**Package.** animation
**What changed.** `flint-animation` moved to `packages/animation/crates/flint-animation` (ADR 0139), with the `animator` component, the `animation` and `tween` script modules, `FieldTweens` (out of flint-script) and, until #1621, skeletons and IK. It is on unless `[packages]` writes `animation = false`, which also drops the new `flipbook` package. The `animation` game-state policy is the package's: it is still a name any state may use. For a game's Rust: the frame gains `Stage::Tweens`, `Stage::Animate` and `Stage::Pose` (a game extension hears them), `StageCtx::bus` and `SceneCtx::models`, `Subsystem::late_models`, and `HeadlessGame::set_animation` takes a `flint_package::CapturePose`; `ScriptCommand::Tween` and `ScriptHost::tween` are gone (`tween` is a module command), `GameCore::animation` and `flint_player::HeadlessAnimation` are gone (`flint_animation::player::HeadlessAnimation`), and `call_sprite_anim_ends` is `call_animation_ends(world, &[(entity, clip)])`.
**What to do.** Nothing for scenes and scripts. A game binary naming one of the Rust items above takes its new name.
