### `flint asset generate` and the ai package's other commands come with the package; `CliExtension` is `flint_package::cli`'s (#1553)
**What changed.** The ai package registers its subcommands of `flint asset` (`generate`, `regenerate`, `validate`, `manifest`, `costs`, `job`) with the CLI launcher as a `CliExtension`, the way a game binary adds its own. The stock `flint` has every one of them, with the same flags. `flint asset --help` now lists them after the kernel's (`import`, `list`, `info`, `resolve`, `where`). A `flint` built without `ai` has none of them in its parser or its help. `flint asset generate` (or `--help` on it) there exits 2 naming the package, as before, and `--json` carries `package` in its error document. `CliExtension` gains `under`, the engine command an extension's commands go under (`subcommands(..).under(&["asset"])`). The trait and the output helpers moved to `flint_package::cli`, and `flint_cli::extension` re-exports them. `flint_cli::run_with` now puts `flint_cli::stock()`, the extensions of every package the build has, ahead of yours.
**What to do.** Nothing. A game binary written against ADR 0133 compiles unchanged and keeps the packages' commands. A script that read `flint asset --help` for the order of the subcommands should read the names instead.
