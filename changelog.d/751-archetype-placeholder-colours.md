### An archetype sets its placeholder box's colour: `[archetype.<name>.visual]` (#751)
**What changed.** An entity with a `bounds` box and nothing else to draw takes its colour from its archetype's new `visual` table (`color = [r, g, b]` or `[r, g, b, a]`, `wireframe = true|false`). The renderer no longer knows `room`, `door`, `furniture`, `character`, `wall`, `floor`, `ceiling` or `pillar` by name: their colours moved into their own schema files, unchanged, so a scene that uses them renders as before. An archetype with no `visual` draws grey, as a game's did already. A malformed `bounds` (a `min` or `max` that isn't three numbers) now draws a 1 m box instead of the archetype's old built-in size. `flint_render::ArchetypeVisual` and `SceneRenderer::set_archetype_visual` are gone; `flint_schema::ArchetypeVisual` and `SceneBinding::with_archetypes(&registry)` replace them.
**What to do.** Give your own archetypes a `[archetype.<name>.visual]` if their boxes should not be grey. A game binary that embeds `PlayerApp` needs nothing. Rust that binds a scene itself adds `.with_archetypes(&registry)` to its `SceneBinding`.
