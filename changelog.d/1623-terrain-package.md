### Terrain is a standard package: `terrain = false` drops the terrain, its editor and `terrain_height` (#1623)
**Package.** terrain
**What changed.** `flint-terrain` moved to `packages/terrain/crates/flint-terrain` (ADR 0138 decision 5), with the `terrain` component and archetype, the `terrain` script module (`terrain_height`) and the terrain editor (`flint edit x.terrain.toml`, `flint terrain-edit`). It is on unless `[packages]` writes `terrain = false`: then neither launcher links the crate, a scene's `terrain` is `unlisted-package`, and procgen's `terrain_v1` (`flint gen x.terrain.toml`) goes with it, since it is procgen's part *with* terrain. For a game's Rust: `GroundField` and the plain `Heightmap` are `flint_geometry`'s (`ground`, `heightmap`; still re-exported by `flint_player` and `flint_terrain`); a `Heightmap`'s `from_png`, `save_png` and `sample_world` are the `flint_terrain::HeightmapFiles` trait's; `ScriptSystem::set_terrain_height_fn` is gone (the package samples the ground itself); and the engine runs a package's *scene binder* right after `bind_scene` (`Registry::scene_binder`, `flint_package::bind`), which is how the terrain is built in the player, the viewer and `flint render`.
**What to do.** Nothing for scenes and scripts. A game binary that called `Heightmap::from_png` or `save_png` adds `use flint_terrain::HeightmapFiles;`. A 2D or indoor game can write `terrain = false` under `[packages]`.
