### A terrain recipe can say where the land is: `op = "falloff"` (#1694)
**Package.** terrain
**What changed.** `[[height_layers]]` takes `op = "falloff"`: a shape over the map (`shape = "island" | "edge" | "radial" | "gradient"`, with `center`, `radius`, `width`, `curve`, `direction`, `invert`) that keeps the heights so far inside it and sinks them outside (`blend = "sink"`, the default; also `"multiply"` and `"add"` with `amplitude`). An `island` falloff after the noise bakes the map's border to height 0 for any seed, so an island is a recipe, not a seed search, and land no longer meets the map's edge in a cut cliff. The terrain editor's generate mode has a **+ Falloff** button and the layer's fields; `flint gen` checks the new keys against `terrain_v1`'s schema. Specs without a falloff bake the same bytes as before.
**What to do.** Nothing. A recipe that searched seeds for an island, or got its regions from a low-frequency layer with `blend = "multiply"`, can use a falloff instead (the book's Terrain page, "Saying where the land is").
