### A script's top level runs once, with the world there: `let door = get_entity("door");` works (#692)
**What changed.** A script's top-level statements used to run at load with no world (a top-level `get_entity` gave `-1`, `self_entity()` gave `0`), and then again before every callback, so a top-level `log`, `play_sound` or `seed_random` ran once per callback per frame. Now the top level runs once, just before the scene's first `on_init` (or the first callback after a chunk loads the script), with the world lent and `self_entity()` the script's own entity. Callbacks no longer re-run it; only the top-level `import`s are evaluated again, from a cache. A hot reload runs the new source's top level once, with the world. For Rust hosts: `ScriptEngine::reload_script(world, id, ast)` and `ScriptSync::check_hot_reload(engine, world)` take the world.
**What to do.** Nothing for most games. A script that leaned on the re-run (a top-level counter or `log` meant to fire every frame) moves that into `on_update`. A host calling `reload_script` or `check_hot_reload` passes its world.
