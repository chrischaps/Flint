### `reveal_invert = true` keeps a style outside its reveal region, so the plain picture can open from a point (#1180)
**What changed.** Every style's table (and `--style`, `set_post_style_param`, F4's Reveal group) takes `reveal_invert`. Inverted, the region still grows with `reveal` and keeps its shape, edge, ink, rim and shadow, but the style is kept outside it and the frame it read shows through it: `--style ascii:reveal=0.3,reveal_shape=iris,reveal_invert=true` is the rendered picture in a circle with glyphs round it. Inverted, `reveal = 0` is the style everywhere and `reveal = 1` the live frame, byte for byte the frames of a plain reveal at 1 and 0; easing `reveal` 0 to 1 opens the plain picture from `reveal_x`, `reveal_y`. The rim of bare paper stays on the style's side and the shadow falls into the hole. `reveal_invert` is a reserved key, so a user style can no longer name a parameter or option `reveal_invert`. A reveal without the key draws exactly as before.
**What to do.** Nothing. A film that wiped the plain picture in because an iris shrank the wrong way can use `reveal_shape = "iris"` with `reveal_invert = true`.
