### A terrain names its recipe: `terrain.spec` bakes a `.terrain.toml` at load and again when it's saved (#1697)
**Package.** terrain
**What changed.** `[entities.x.terrain] spec = "land/hills.terrain.toml"` makes the terrain from its recipe: the spec is baked at load through a cache keyed by what the bake reads (in memory, and in `.flint/procgen-cache/` or `FLINT_PROCGEN_CACHE`), and its `[geometry]`, `[textures]` and `[grass]` are the terrain's settings, a key written on the component winning. `seed` bakes it at another seed. The pixels are those of the PNGs `flint gen` bakes from the same recipe. Saving the spec while `flint play` runs rebakes it off the frame thread and rebuilds the chunks, grass, physics heightfield and `terrain_height`. `flint check` warns `overrides-spec` for a component key that disagrees with its recipe (or a `heightmap` standing in for the bake) and says `bad-terrain-spec` for a recipe that doesn't parse. The `terrain` component and archetype schemas no longer write defaults into a scene; the readers' defaults are the numbers they were (256 x 256 x 50, chunks of 64, tile 16, roughness 0.85, the grass's), so a scene without a spec draws as before. A scene that wrote `layer0_texture = ""` beside a spec now gets no texture for that layer.
**What to do.** Nothing. To drop a manual bake, replace `heightmap`, `splat_map` and the copied settings with `spec`, move the layer textures into the spec's `[textures.layers.layerN] path` (from the spec's folder) and the grass into its `[grass]`, then `flint check` the scene.
