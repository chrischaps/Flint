### Splat rules take `slope_min`, slopes are rise over run, and `[splat] fallback` says what no rule covers (#1696)
**Package.** terrain
**What changed.** A `[[splat_rules]]` entry takes `slope_min`, the gentlest slope it paints, its weight fading in above it, so rock can go on steep ground and only steep ground. Slopes are now measured as rise over run (1 is 45 degrees), as the docs and schema always said; the bake used to measure the sine of the angle, which never passes 1, so `slope_max = 1.0` was no limit and values over 1 were all the same. `slope_max` left out is now no ceiling (it was 1.0). A new `[splat]` table's `fallback` decides what a texel no rule covers gets: a layer (0, as before, when the table is left out) or `"none"`, weights of 0 that grow no grass. A height band that reaches 0 or 1 now paints the lowest texel and the summit, which used to fall to the fallback. `flint gen` adds how much of the map no rule covered to its coverage line. The terrain editor's rules have Slope Min and Slope Max boxes and an Uncovered choice. `terrain_v1`'s revision is 2, so maps a project registered are baked again.
**What to do.** Re-bake any `.terrain.toml` with slope rules (`flint gen x.terrain.toml`) and look at its steep ground: a `slope_max` now cuts off at the angle it names (`atan`: 0.5 is 27 degrees, 1.0 is 45, 2.0 is 63). A rule that existed only to keep layer 0 off unseen ground (a sea-bed rule) can go, with `[splat] fallback = "none"` in its place.
